## SECTION 2: ABOUT

### Version A — Professional / Precise
*(Good for: international clients, technical referrals, RFP contexts. Tone: credential-forward, rigorous, confident.)*

My path to ML consulting started with language, not code.

I completed my PhD in Hispanic Linguistics at the University of Iowa, where I spent years studying how humans encode meaning — how context shapes interpretation, and how organizations talk past each other even when they think they're communicating clearly. My MA work overlapped directly with Natural Language Processing, which put me at the intersection of computational and human systems earlier than most data engineers get there.

After finishing my doctorate, I moved into data engineering and then into senior consulting roles — building cloud-based data pipelines in Microsoft Fabric, designing ML-informed systems, and creating Power BI dashboards that give executives real-time visibility without requiring an analyst in the room. I've worked with advertising agencies in New York, wealth management firms, and companies on both sides of the Atlantic.

What my technical counterparts rarely bring to the table: the research training to diagnose *why* data isn't being used, not just how to display it better. That's where strategy begins.

I work in English and Spanish, and split my time between St. Louis, Missouri and Valencia, Spain.

I started my career trying to understand how people make meaning — which sounds philosophical until you realize it's exactly what every data project is actually about.

# NOTES FOR REVIEW

## The two About versions — what to decide
- **Version A** positions you as a rigorous technologist with an unusual academic background. A prospect reading this thinks: "She's serious, she has the credentials, she's probably expensive."
- **Version B** positions you as a trusted advisor who happens to be technical. A prospect reading this thinks: "She gets it, I want to meet her, let's talk."
- A third option: open with Version B warmth, close with Version A credential anchors. Hybrid is often the right answer.

## The hero headline
"Most data problems are communication problems. I fix both." — this earns attention because it's unexpected from a data engineer. The linguistics PhD makes it land without feeling like a gimmick. If it feels too clever, the fallback is: "I help companies turn data and machine learning into decisions that actually stick."

## What's deliberately left out
- No pricing (you're past that)
- No "dashboards in a week" framing
- No package tiers
- The portfolio template/DevFolio attribution

## Where Soul Meets Systems
The newsletter name is genuinely strong — it describes the whole positioning in four words. It should appear in the footer/contact section as a quiet closer, not a hard sell.

## Spanish version
When ready to add a Spanish version, the About (Version B) translates most naturally — the warmth reads as genuine in Spanish rather than as marketing copy. Version A reads more formally in Spanish, which has its own advantages for certain client types.
