About

Most data problems are language problems.

I know this because I spent years studying language before I ever wrote a pipeline. My PhD is in Hispanic Linguistics — I was trained to notice how organizations use words, how context changes meaning, and how people talk past each other while believing they're communicating. It turns out that's exactly the diagnostic skill you need when a company's data infrastructure isn't working.

After my doctorate, I moved into data engineering and ML consulting. I've built cloud pipelines in Microsoft Fabric, implemented LLM and RAG systems for companies that couldn't yet hire in-house AI teams, and designed decision tools for leadership at firms in New York, St. Louis, and across Spain. My MA work overlapped with Natural Language Processing — so the shift from linguistics research to ML implementation was less of a pivot than it sounds.

What I bring that most technical consultants don't: I can walk into a room, listen to how a team talks about their data problem, and tell you in thirty minutes whether it's a technical problem, a governance problem, or a communication problem — and which one to fix first.

I work in English and Spanish, and split my time between St. Louis, Missouri and Valencia, Spain.

A few projects worth knowing about

Executive Visibility — National Retailer

A large American retailer needed regional managers to make staffing and inventory decisions without routing every question through the analytics team. The real problem wasn't the data — reporting was built for analysts, not operators. I rebuilt the decision layer: a dashboard correlating review data and sales performance across locations, readable in thirty seconds.

Power BI · Data modeling · Stakeholder research

Client Experience Gaps — Wealth Management Firm

A wealth management firm had survey data about client satisfaction but no clear view of where advisors and clients were seeing things differently. I ran a user research phase first — because the mental model mattered as much as the visualization — then built a dashboard surfacing the gaps between what advisors believed clients felt and what the data actually showed.

Power BI · Survey analysis · Qualitative research

App Adoption — Internal Tool Redesign

A company's employees weren't using their mobile app. The assumption internally was a training problem. The reality was a language and layout problem: the interface used terms that didn't match how field workers thought about their jobs. I conducted user research, identified the friction, and redesigned the key interface. Adoption increased measurably.

UX research · Product thinking · Information architecture
